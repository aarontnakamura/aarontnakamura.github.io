Exploring how experiences shape the way we perceive and interpret the world.

I’m Aaron Nakamura, a Ph.D. student in psychology/cognitive neuroscience at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa. My research examines how lived experiences shape the way we attend to, perceive, and interpret the world. I’m especially interested in why people respond differently to similar experiences, how they adapt over time, and how these differences appear across cognitive, behavioral, and neural levels.

Research Areas

My research focuses on how lived experiences shape the way people think, perceive, and behave—and why those effects differ from person to person.

  • 01 Childhood adversity, adaptation, and resilience
  • 02 ABC-P: Attention, behavior, cognition, and perception
  • 03 Neural plasticity and functional connectivity · fMRI / EEG / ERP
  • 04 Cultural identity development and language acquisition
